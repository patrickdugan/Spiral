In-and-Out-Burger

Pay in to the burger's address. A zero-knowledge proof shows the payment confirmed. A BitVM vault then pays the bond out to a fresh key derived from yours, one only you can spend. A fancy way of getting your money back.

The order, on testnet4

Every transaction below is on the public testnet4 chain. Confirmations are read live from mempool.space tip …

    Three claims, one verdict each

    Three vaults, three claims against the same order. The garbled verifier decides; Bitcoin enforces what follows.

    Your money back, at a key only you can spend

    The payer revealed a public key P when they paid. The payout goes to P′ = P + t·G, with t = SHA-256 tagged hash of the payment outpoint and P. Only the payer knows p, so only they can spend at p + t.

    What Bitcoin refused

    Counterexamples run against Bitcoin Core 31.1. Each was refused for the reason shown, and none of them pays P′.

    What you are trusting

    Bitcoin enforces

    • Only the claimant starts a claim, and the claim commits to the statement, the garbled circuit and the payout script.
    • The payout goes to P′ and nowhere else: the desk's presignature covers its outputs.
    • The payout waits 6 blocks after the claim.
    • Only this claim's reject label can spend the disprove leaf.

    Trusted setup (this demo)

    • The desk presigns the graph for each order, a committee of one, in place of a covenant.
    • Nobody checks the garbled tables are a correct garbling of M14 (no cut-and-choose).
    • The input labels of the proof are handed over off-chain.
    • The Nova chain starts from a checkpoint at block 160,000 that the desk pins against its own node, not from genesis.
