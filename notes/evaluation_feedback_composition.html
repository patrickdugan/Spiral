1. The executable record

src/spiral_ln/evaluation_feedback_composition.py implements \(\mathcal E\) as a frozen record: six game.* fields (\(G\): legal actions, revealed-or-hidden resource, utility, budget, horizon, carry map, for the two-location game played once per item); pi (\(\Pi\): observation-to-action lookup plus per-item overrides, so a patch memorizes an item identifier); items (\(D\): s0,s1 in selection set S, r0,r1 in readout set R); acquisition (\(Z\): privileged record, action redacted on named sets); judgment (\(J\): one for the resource, inverted on flipped items, less a cost for choosing right; undefined on a redacted record); generation (\(g\)); declarations (role and access class per set). \(\mathcal A\) enters only in Proposition 4; \(C\) is not modeled.

Each operator is a pure function with a declared footprint. Restrict, Grant, Cap, Hide (player-facing), Observe (observation-only), Controller, Rescore, Payoff (optionally also installing the utility as the judgment) and Repeat write the fields their names suggest and read at most what they write. \(\operatorname{Feedback}_{S,q}\) reads game.legal, game.revealed, pi, items, acquisition, judgment, declarations, generation and writes pi, generation: item-level access returns the per-item vector and overrides each item judged nonpositive with the other action; score-only returns the sum for the incumbent and three fixed candidates and keeps the first maximizer; sealed returns nothing and only advances \(g\). Feedback on a set whose declaration forbids it raises: ill-typed, not a breach.

2. The disjoint-update law and its witnesses

Proposition 1 (disjoint-update law). Call a footprint sound if the operator changes no field outside \(W\) and the new values of \(W\) are a function of the old values of \(R\). If \(a,b\) have sound footprints with \(W_a\cap(R_b\cup W_b)=\emptyset\) and \(W_b\cap(R_a\cup W_a)=\emptyset\), then \(a;b=b;a\) on every record.

Proof. Fix \(x\). Fields outside \(W_a\cup W_b\) are untouched in both orders. For \(f\in W_a\): in \(a;b\), \(a\) writes \(f\) from \(x|_{R_a}\) and \(b\) leaves it (\(f\notin W_b\)); in \(b;a\), \(b\) leaves \(R_a\) unchanged (\(W_b\cap R_a=\emptyset\)), so \(a\) writes the same value. Likewise \(W_b\). \(\square\)

Boundary: sufficient only, and about the record, not about additivity of behavioural effects (calculus §5). Fixture: footprint_audit checks soundness of all 13 footprints on the 384 enumerated records (write side per record; read side by grouping records with equal \(R\)-projection and requiring equal \(W\)-images); commutation_audit checks every disjoint pair on every record. Evidence: algebraic identity (the proposition); finite verified example (the model's footprints are sound; the instances hold).

Proposition 2 (commutation table). On the 384 records, of the 78 off-diagonal unordered pairs of the 13 operators, 60 have disjoint footprints and commute, 15 do not commute, and 3 commute despite overlapping footprints. The non-commuting pairs, with the field in which the two orders differ:

Pair Differ in First witness (receipt)
Grant\(_2\), Cap\(_5\) game.budget budget 4: grant-then-cap 5, cap-then-grant 6 (calculus §5)
Rescore\(_j\), Feedback (item, score) pi only always-left, flip s1: rescore-then-feedback learns nothing; feedback-then-rescore memorizes s1
Observe (redact S), Feedback (item, score) pi only observe-then-feedback learns nothing; feedback-then-observe memorizes s1

Payoff without training commutes with Feedback (it writes only game.utility), as do Grant, Cap and Repeat; Repeat commuting at design time says nothing about the aggregation unit (Proposition 4). Commuting despite overlap: sealed Feedback with either other Feedback (both write generation; \(g+1+1\) is order-free) and the two Payoffs (same utility). Rescore before Feedback changes what the loop learns; after, only the readout: the final \(J\) agrees, \(\Pi\) differs, and \(V^S\) is 1 against 1/2 in the hand-computed test. Evidence: finite verified example, for these records and update rules only.

3. Typing as ledger audit

compile_ledger maps a design expression with declarations to the (sets, events) input of audit_ledger: item-level Feedback records one item_feedback naming every item of the set (the vector reached the loop, whatever the rule did with it); score-only records one score_query per policy scored; sealed records nothing; Readout\(_t\) records one readout; other operators are outside the channel. \(\Gamma\) carries declarations, cumulative patches, retirement and readouts per set:

  • (F-SEL) \(\Gamma\vdash\operatorname{Feedback}_{s,q}\), \(q>\)sealed, iff \(s\) is declared selection, access\((s)\ge q\), and \(s\) not retired; item-level adds \(|s|\) to patches\((s)\), and \(s\) retires once patches\(/|s|\) exceeds the bound (the crossing step is itself typed).
  • (F-SEALED) \(\operatorname{Feedback}_{s,\text{sealed}}\) is typed, \(\Gamma'=\Gamma\); likewise (NEUTRAL) every other operator.
  • (RO) \(\operatorname{Readout}_t\) iff role\((t)=\)readout (hence sealed) and the count stays within the readout budget.

Proposition 3. Over all 22,143 sequences of length \(\le4\) from the nine-symbol alphabet {Feedback\(_{S,q}\), Feedback\(_{R,q}\) for the three access classes, Readout\(_R\), Readout\(_S\), Rescore} under three declaration contexts (S declared item-level, score-only or sealed; R sealed; budget 2; bound 1 with \(|S|=2\)), a sequence is well-typed iff audit_ledger returns declarations_respected = True (2,581 respected, 11,462 breached, 8,100 raise). Evidence: finite verified example.

Two findings. (i) The ledger has two rejection modes: every sequence containing Readout\(_S\) makes audit_ledger raise ("readout events apply to readout sets only") rather than record a breach, so that run gets no report, unlike the other breaches, which its docstring says should survive into the report; the equivalence counts a raise as not respected. (ii) The literal rule "Feedback\(_{s,q}\) requires role selection and access \(\ge q\)" is too strict at \(q=\)sealed: a sealed read has no event, so Feedback\(_{R,\text{sealed}}\) is invisible to the ledger; with the literal premise the equivalence fails on exactly 1,414 sequences, all respected runs containing it and nothing else ill-typed. (F-SEALED) drops the premise; audit_ledger is not patched.

4. Repeat with a carry map

Proposition 4 (generations joined by Feedback are not blocks). Let \(K\ge2\) candidates each succeed independently with probability \(p\in(0,1)\) on each of \(n\) items of \(s\); generation 1 keeps the best by score (\(S_1\)); the carry map of \(\operatorname{Repeat}_{2,m}\) carries \(\Pi_2\) into generation 2's pool with \(K-1\) fresh candidates (\(S_2\)). Then \(S_2\) is the maximum of \(2K-1\) i.i.d. scores, so \(\mathbb E S_2=p+\gamma(n,2K-1,p)>p+\gamma(n,K,p)=\mathbb E S_1\) with \(\gamma\) the gap of selection_inflation; \(S_2\ge S_1\) pointwise with \(\Pr[S_2>S_1]\ge(1-p)^{nK}\bigl(1-(1-p)^{n(K-1)}\bigr)>0\); hence \((S_1,S_2)\) is not exchangeable, and the mean over generations exceeds what the same \(\mathcal A\) reports over independent blocks by \((\gamma(n,2K-1,p)-\gamma(n,K,p))/2>0\), while the sealed readout stays at \(p\): the whole expected gain \(\mathbb E[S_2-S_1]\) is a change in \(\rho\). Under the reset map \(S_1,S_2\) are i.i.d. and the two aggregations agree.

Proof. The kept candidate's realized outcomes on \(s\) are fixed, so its generation-2 score is \(S_1\), and the pool maximum is the maximum of the original \(K\) and the fresh \(K-1\) i.i.d. scores. Monotonicity gives \(S_2\ge S_1\); "all \(nK\) generation-1 outcomes fail and some fresh outcome succeeds" has the stated probability and forces \(S_2>S_1\); unequal means rule out exchangeability. \(\square\)

Fixture: repeat_carry_fixture enumerates every outcome matrix. At \(n=1,K=2,p=1/2\): \(\mathbb ES_1=3/4\), \(\mathbb ES_2=7/8\), generation mean \(13/16\) under carry against \(3/4\) over independent blocks, \(\Pr[S_2>S_1]=1/8\), \(\Pr[S_1>S_2]=0\), \(\rho=1/4\) then \(3/8\); three further cells agree with selection_inflation at \(2K-1\). Evidence: algebraic identity under the stated model; finite verified example for the numbers. Boundary: two generations, equal candidates, independent items, a carry map re-scoring the incumbent on the same items; the multi-generation chain is built elsewhere.

5. Readout gap under Rescore

Proposition 5. \(\rho=V^s-V^r\) is a function of \((G,\Pi,D,Z,J)\); \(\operatorname{Rescore}_j\) writes \(J\) only, \(\operatorname{Feedback}\) writes \(\Pi\) and \(g\). So \(\rho\) can change with \(\Pi\), \(g\) fixed and no ledger event, and a change in \(\rho\) alone does not identify feedback. Fixture (readout_gap_rescore_fixture, always-left controller, \(\rho=0\)): rescoring r1 moves \(\rho\) to \(-1/2\); rescoring s1 moves it to \(+1/2\), exactly the \(\rho\) that \(\operatorname{Feedback}_{S,\text{item}}\) produces by memorizing s1. The ledger separates the two (0 against 2 patches on S); \(\rho\) does not. Evidence: algebraic identity (footprints); finite verified example (values).

6. What is general and what is enumerated

Proven in general: Propositions 1, 4 and 5 as stated. Checked only on enumerated cases: footprint soundness (384 records), the commutation table (13 operators, 384 records), the typing/ledger equivalence (22,143 sequences, three contexts), and every number above. The witnesses concern one pair of update rules on one tiny game; another patch or selection rule needs the audit rerun. All laws are design-time transformations of the record, not what a learned controller does with what the channel transmits. No model was run.
