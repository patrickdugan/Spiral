Feedback channel: suggested insertions for the evaluation design calculus

Draft 0.2 — 2026-10-12. Suggestions only. Draft 0.2 states the ceiling law for failure-targeted patching as well as for patching chosen at random.

This file changes nothing in evaluation_design_calculus.md or evaluation_game_testbed.md. Each block names a location in those documents, offers text for it, and states what supports the text. Where a block is adopted, its wording should be made the author's own. Where it is not, the executable fixtures still stand by themselves.

The gap being addressed: the calculus already says that a judgment "shown during play or used for learning ... also enters observations or controller updates" (§2), and that frozen-controller sensitivity differs from sensitivity "after retraining, selecting another controller" (§7). It has no operator for that channel, no estimand for what the channel does to a score, and no record of how often it was used. A self-improvement loop is that channel applied once per generation.

A. §2, after "Maintain three distinctions"

Suggested fourth distinction:

  1. A judgment that reaches whatever produces the next controller is a channel. Its source set, its access class, and the number of times it was used belong in the experiment record. A score measured on a set that fed this channel is a selection score. A capability claim needs a set that did not.

Suggested row:

Suggested sentence beneath the table:

Feedback is not \(\operatorname{Rescore}\), which is offline, and not \(\operatorname{Observe}\), which changes what the evaluator records. It reads \(J\) restricted to \(s\) and writes \(\Pi\) for the next generation. Declaring a set sealed while a score from it reaches the loop is ill-typed.

C. §4, after the sentence on Repeat and reset/carry maps

Generations joined by Feedback are not independent blocks. The carry map carries the controller, and with it whatever of \(s\) the channel transmitted. Aggregating a per-generation score across such generations as if each were a fresh draw of the same experiment mistakes the unit.

D. §5, three laws and one observation

Coarsening law.

The access classes are ordered: sealed is a deterministic function of the score, and the score is a deterministic function of the per-item outcomes. Total-variation distance between two controllers' feedback therefore cannot increase from item-level to score-only to sealed. Two controllers that fail different items with equal totals are separated completely by item-level feedback and not at all by a score.

Support: access_order_fixture; the ordering is checked for every pair of distributions on a rational grid over two-item outcomes.

Selection law.

Keeping the best of \(K\) candidates by score on \(s\), then reporting that score, overstates: \(\mathbb E[\max_k\hat S_k]\ge\max_k\mathbb E[\hat S_k]\). This is the same convexity as the mixture law, with the candidate index in place of the mixture component. For candidates of equal true value one half, two candidates and one item give a reported value of three quarters.

Support: selection_inflation, checked against independent enumeration of every outcome matrix in seven small cells. Exact gaps for two candidates at \(p=\tfrac12\) are \(\tfrac14\), \(\tfrac{35}{256}\), about 0.0700 and about 0.0352 at 1, 4, 16 and 64 items; multiplied by \(\sqrt n\) these are 0.250, 0.273, 0.280 and 0.282. The value \(1/(2\sqrt\pi)\approx0.2821\) is the standard Gaussian figure for the larger of two draws. The fixture verifies the listed cells and no limit.

Boundary: one generation, equal candidates, independent items. Accumulation across generations is a simulation result, not an exact one.

Ceiling law.

With labels flipped independently at rate \(\varepsilon<\tfrac12\), no predictor blind to the flips exceeds \(1-\varepsilon\) in expectation on observed labels. Let \(v\) be the controller's observed accuracy before any item of \(s\) is memorized. If it memorizes a share \(m/n\) of \(s\) chosen without regard to its outcomes, it scores \(m/n+(1-m/n)v\) on \(s\) and crosses the ceiling only when \(m/n>(1-\varepsilon-v)/(1-v)\). If it memorizes items it failed, which is what item-level feedback supplies, it scores \(v+m/n\) and crosses when \(m/n>1-\varepsilon-v\): the same threshold \((1-\varepsilon-v)/(1-v)\), now as a share of the failed items. Below the threshold a ceiling check is silent.

Support: ceiling_detection_threshold and targeted_ceiling_detection_threshold. With clean accuracy \(\tfrac45\) and \(\varepsilon=\tfrac1{10}\): \(v=\tfrac{37}{50}\). Memorizing at random, the check is silent below \(\tfrac8{13}\) of the set. Patching failures, it is silent below \(\tfrac4{25}\) of the set, which is \(\tfrac8{13}\) of the failed items. Both formulas assume a memorized item has no effect on any other item. The nearest-neighbour learner in the second simulation did not satisfy that, and it also did not reliably absorb a patch: at \(n=4000\) it patched a quarter of the set and its reported score stayed at 0.759, below the 0.90 ceiling.

Observation on what item-level feedback selects.

An item the controller fails is more likely than an average item to carry a wrong label, whenever the controller is better than chance: \(\Pr[\text{flipped}\mid\text{failed}]=\varepsilon a/(\varepsilon a+(1-\varepsilon)(1-a))\), which exceeds \(\varepsilon\) exactly when \(a>\tfrac12\). Feeding failures back concentrates label noise in what the next controller is built from.

Support: failed_item_noise_share; \(\tfrac4{13}\) against a base rate of \(\tfrac1{10}\) in the fixture. In the second simulation the measured share among patched items was 20–29% against the same 10% base rate. This is the reason the unchecked answer in the Proposer–Solver recipe (atlas §11) matters once its output is fed back.

Suggested closing sentence, matching the section's existing last line:

No unconditional claim is made that feedback lowers readout performance. In the simulations, score-only selection of fresh training batches raised it.

E. §7, after the definition of the order effect

For a controller \(\pi_g\) produced with feedback from \(s\), and a sealed set \(r\) from the same sampling law, define the readout gap \(\rho_g=V^{s}_{\pi_g}-V^{r}_{\pi_g}\). It compares two measurements of one controller, not two designs, so it is not a design difference. A gain series computed on \(s\) estimates the change in value plus the change in \(\rho\).

Suggested section, placed after "Executable confirmation and adversarial checks":

Feedback channel and multi-generation ledger

evaluation_game_feedback.py extends the single split enforced by confirm() to a sequence of generations. Sets are declared with a role and an access class. Events record a score reaching the loop, items reaching the loop, or an experimenter reading a sealed set. Malformed declarations and malformed events raise: a readout set not declared sealed, identifiers shared across sets, or a readout event naming a selection set (a readout is by definition a read of a sealed set; a selection set is read through score queries). Breaches of a well-formed declaration are recorded instead, and move the readout endpoint to not_evaluated: a breach is a finding about the run. A sealed read transmits nothing and is not an event, so Feedback at sealed access is always well-typed. The composition note checks that, under these rules, well-typed sequences are exactly those the ledger reports as respected, on every sequence of length at most four over a nine-operator alphabet. Reads of the sealed set are budgeted, since a stop-or-continue decision taken on a readout is itself adaptive. Identifiers detect recorded overlap. They do not certify independence.

H. What is not delivered

  • Feedback is not implemented against hive_lab adapters or any external policy. The module checks declarations and arithmetic.
  • No loop with a language model has been run. The first observed-controller study would pair a small model with a declared selection set and a sealed readout, and report the ledger.
  • Partial leakage, where the loop learns failure categories and not items, is now treated in evaluation_feedback_lattice.md: category channels form a lattice of partial leaks, with the cost of each position exact. Composition with the other operators is in evaluation_feedback_composition.md, and the selection gap across generations in evaluation_feedback_multigen.md.
  • The simulations fix one parameter set each. No sweep over candidate count, decay rate, or label-noise rate was run.
