The selection gap across generations, exactly

Draft 0.5 — 2026-10-13 (0.4–0.2: 2026-10-13; 0.1: 2026-10-12). Companion to evaluation_feedback_insertions.md, whose selection law stops at one generation and whose multi-generation rows come from seeded JavaScript. This note replaces the accumulation claim with a finite Markov chain in exact arithmetic. No model is called; nothing here is evidence about any controller. Evidence levels follow the calculus card (§11): algebraic identity (every parameter), finite verified example (enumerated cells only), synthetic mechanism result (a property of this toy chain). 0.2 added Props. 5–7; 0.3 proved the naive chain above every gated chain in true value (Prop. 8); 0.4 proved the count ordering (Prop. 9) and recorded why the standard routes fail; 0.5 asks whether both survive mixed gain laws (§6): no counterexample in an exact search over every rule, the proof extended two below the target, finite beyond. 1. The chain: vertices, edges, policy

A reused set of \(n\) items. State: the incumbent's realized count \(c\in\{0,\dots,n\}\) on that set. Each generation draws \(K\) candidates i.i.d. and independent of the incumbent, \(C_k\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)\), \(M=\max_k C_k\). Actions: accept or reject the best candidate. Policy: accept iff \(M>c+t(c)\), \(t:\{0..n\}\to\mathbb N\). Edges: \(c\to j\) with probability \(P(M=j)\) for \(j>c+t(c)\); the self-loop carries \(F(c+t(c))^K\), \(F\) the binomial CDF with \(F(j)=1\) for \(j\ge n\). The graph is a DAG of upward edges plus self-loops; its sinks \(\{c:c+t(c)\ge n\}\) are the absorbing states. Initial state \(c_0\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)\). Reported score \(c_G/n\); readout value \(p\); readout gap \(\rho_G=\mathbb E[c_G]/n-p\). In the calculus: \(\operatorname{Repeat}_G\) of one generation (carry map "keep \(c\)") composed with \(\operatorname{Feedback}_{s,\text{score-only}}\), whose consumer selects among candidates.

Gate discretization. sim.js accepts iff \(M>c+z\sqrt{p(1-p)/n}\,n\) with \(p=c/n\), i.e. \(M-c>z\sqrt{c(n-c)/n}\). The difference is an integer \(d\), and \(d>x\) iff \(d\ge\lfloor x\rfloor+1\), so \(t(c)=\lfloor z\sqrt{c(n-c)/n}\rfloor\), computed exactly as \(\lfloor\sqrt{a/b}\rfloor=\mathrm{isqrt}(ab)\,//\,b\). For \(n=16,z=3\): \(t=(0,2,3,4,5,5,5,5,6,5,5,5,5,4,3,2,0)\). Fixture gate_threshold, checked by \(t^2\le z^2c(n-c)/n<(t+1)^2\) on every state and against the real-valued rule for every integer \(d\). Algebraic identity.

2. Naive reuse is a running maximum

Proposition 1. With \(t\equiv0\), \(c_G=\max(c_0,M_1,\dots,M_G)\), the maximum of \(GK+1\) i.i.d. \(\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)\) draws, so \(\rho_G=\) selection_inflation(n, GK+1, p).readout_gap. The initial incumbent is one draw because it is scored on the reused set like every candidate; a known count \(c_0\) gives \(\mathbb E[\max(c_0,X)]\), \(X\) the max of \(GK\) draws (running_max_from_point). Proof. If \(M_g>c_{g-1}\) the policy sets \(c_g=M_g=\max(c_{g-1},M_g)\); otherwise \(c_g=c_{g-1}=\max(c_{g-1},M_g)\). Induct. Algebraic identity. The DP equals the closed form on all 18 null cells (\(n\in\{4,8,16\}\), \(K\in\{1,2,4\}\), \(p\in\{\tfrac12,\tfrac14\}\), \(G=0..20\)); at \(n=2,K=2,G=2\) it equals literal enumeration of all \(2^{10}\) item outcomes. Consequences. (i) \(\rho_G\) is non-decreasing in \(G\): a maximum over more draws is pointwise larger. (ii) Increments are non-increasing: \(a_{m+1}-a_m=\mathbb E[(X-M_m)^+]\) with \(M_m\) pointwise non-decreasing in \(m\). (iii) \(P(c_G=n)=1-(1-p^n)^{GK+1}\), so for fixed \(n\) and \(0<p<1\), \(\rho_G\uparrow1-p\): bounded by the lattice, never stationary. Algebraic identities; the fixture verifies them on the enumerated cells only.

Proposition 5 (bounds). For every \(n,K,p,G\), with \(m=GK+1\) and \(\sigma^2=np(1-p)\), \[\rho_G\le\sqrt{\tfrac{\ln m}{2n}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad \rho_G\le\sqrt{\tfrac{2p(1-p)\ln m}{n}}+\tfrac{\ln m}{3n}.\] Proof. \(Y_i=X_i-np\) is a sum of \(n\) centred Bernoullis in intervals of length one, so Hoeffding's lemma and independence give \(\mathbb E e^{\lambda Y_i}\le e^{\lambda^2n/8}\); the maximal inequality \(\mathbb E\max_iY_i\le\lambda^{-1}\ln\sum_i \mathbb E e^{\lambda Y_i}\) (Jensen, then \(\max\le\sum\)) gives \((\ln m)/\lambda+\lambda n/8\), which \(\lambda=\sqrt{8\ln m/n}\) makes \(\sqrt{n\ln m/2}\); divide by \(n\). For the second bound, a centred \(|Z|\le1\) with variance \(v\) has \(\ln\mathbb E e^{\lambda Z}\le v(e^\lambda-1-\lambda)\le\lambda^2v/(2(1-\lambda/3))\) for \(\lambda<3\); summed over items \(v=\sigma^2\), and \(\lambda^{-1}=\tfrac13+\sqrt{\sigma^2/(2\ln m)}\) in the same maximal inequality gives \(\sqrt{2\sigma^2\ln m}+\tfrac13\ln m\) (Boucheron–Lugosi–Massart, Cor. 2.6). Algebraic identity. Both grow without limit in \(G\) while \(\rho_G\le1-p\), so they are loose at fixed \(n\) for large \(G\) (Hoeffding passes \(1-p\) at \(G=7,4,2\) for \(n=4,p=\tfrac12,K=1,2,4\)). At \(p=\tfrac12\) Hoeffding equals the Gaussian scale \(\sqrt{2p(1-p)\ln m/n}\) of the old remark and Bernstein exceeds it by \(\ln m/(3n)\); Bernstein is tighter on 3 of 360 cell-generations only (\(p=\tfrac14,n=16,m\le3\)). Finite table (bound_fixture, 18 cells \(\times\) 21 generations): both bounds hold everywhere; \(\rho_G\) over the Gaussian scale runs from 0.453 to 0.886 at \(G\in\{1,5,10,20\}\), rising in \(G\) and \(n\): an upper envelope, not an approximation.

3. Fresh blocks

Proposition 2. (a) If each generation scores incumbent and candidates on a fresh block and reports on a second fresh block, the expected reported score is \(p\) at every generation: the readout block is independent of the selection, and under the null every controller has probability \(p\). (b) If it reports on the selection block, the reported count at \(g\ge1\) is \(M\) if \(M>I+t(I)\), else \(I\), with \(I\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)\) the incumbent's fresh count; the law does not depend on \(g\). For \(t\equiv0\) it is \(\max(I,M)\), the maximum of \(K+1\) draws, so the gap equals selection_inflation(n, K+1, p) at every \(g\ge1\) and does not accumulate. Both algebraic identities (independence, linearity); verified on 12 cells and by enumeration at \(n=2,K=2\) in both modes. The reused chain coincides with (b) at \(G=1\) for any gate and exceeds it strictly for \(G\ge2\) on every enumerated cell (finite check).

4. The gate

Proposition 3 (false acceptance). From state \(c\) the generation accepts with probability \(\alpha(c)=1-F(\min(n,c+t(c)))^K\); under the null every acceptance is false, and the unconditional rate at generation \(g\) is \(\sum_c\pi_{g-1}(c)\alpha(c)\). Algebraic identity. For \(n=16,K=4,z=3\): \(\alpha(8)=1-(1-17/65536)^4\approx1.0\times10^{-3}\); naive \(\approx0.36\).

Proposition 4 (gate dominance, null only). For any \(t\ge0\), couple the gated and naive chains on the same draws. The gated state always belongs to \(\{c_0,M_1,\dots,M_g\}\), whose maximum is the naive state; hence \(c^t_G\le c^0_G\) pointwise, \(\rho^t_G\le\rho^0_G\), and \(\alpha^t(c)\le\alpha^0(c)\). Algebraic identity by coupling, under the null hypothesis that every candidate's law is history-free; with true gains it fails and can reverse (§6). Values at \(G=20,p=\tfrac12,n=16,K=4\): naive \(0.2947\); \(z=1\): \(0.2723\); \(z=2\): \(0.2019\); \(z=3\): \(0.1328\); expected false acceptances \(2.34\to0.33\). \(n=8,K=2\): naive \(0.3645\), \(z=3\): \(0.0967\). The gate also creates sinks below the ceiling (\(n=8,z=3\): states \(4..8\) absorb, a gated loop freezes), behind the simulation's GATED arm accepting 0–2% of generations.

5. Correlated candidates: the mutation chain

sim.js perturbs the incumbent's per-item quirks, correlating each candidate with the incumbent item by item. Exact model: on each item, independently, a candidate copies the incumbent's outcome w.p. \(1-q\) and redraws it as Bernoulli(\(p\)) w.p. \(q\). Marginally a candidate is still \(\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)\), with item correlation \(1-q\). The map from \(\sin\phi=0.2\) to \(q\) is not exact (the rotation acts on latent quirks, not outcomes, and no \(q\) matches its conditional count law).

Proposition 6. Given incumbent count \(c\), a candidate's count is \(c-L+U\) with \(L\sim\mathrm{Bin}(c,q(1-p))\), \(U\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n-c,qp)\) independent (a right item is lost iff redrawn and failing, a wrong one gained iff redrawn and succeeding); by exchangeability and item-symmetry the law depends on the incumbent's outcome vector only through \(c\), so with \(K\) conditionally i.i.d. candidates the process is again a Markov chain on \(c\) (mutation_candidate_law, mutation_transition_kernel). (a) \(q=1\) gives \(\mathrm{Bin}(c,p)+\mathrm{Bin}(n-c,p)=\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)\): the independent chain, exactly. (b) \(q=0\) gives the point mass at \(c\): nothing is ever accepted, \(\rho_G=0\). (c) For every \(q\), acceptance requires \(M>c+t(c)\ge c\), so \(c_g\) is pointwise non-decreasing, \(\rho_G\) is non-decreasing in \(G\), and \(\rho_G\le1-p\). (d) From \(c=n-1\) a candidate reaches \(n\) with probability \(qp(1-q(1-p))^{n-1}\), maximal at \(q=1/(n(1-p))\) and decreasing beyond it, so the kernel is not monotone in \(q\) state by state and no coupling in \(q\) exists. All algebraic identities; (a) is checked as equality of the whole state law on every null cell (naive and \(z=1\)), (b)–(d) on the same cells, and the DP matches enumeration of outcome vectors at \(n=2,K=2,G=2\) and \(n=3,K=2,G=1\) (testing the Markov-on-count claim itself). Proposition 1 has no analogue: the candidate law moves with the state.

Monotonicity in \(q\), finite survey (\(q\in\{0,\tfrac14,\tfrac12,\tfrac34,1\}\), 18 null cells, naive and \(z=1\)). \(\rho_1\) is non-decreasing in \(q\) on every cell: correlation lowers the first-generation inflation. \(\rho_G\) is not monotone in \(q\) on any naive cell (first failure at \(G=2\)–5) and on 15 of 18 gated cells; at \(G=20\) every \(q<1\) gives a larger gap than \(q=1\) on every naive cell with \(n\ge8\), and \(q=\tfrac12\) does on all 18 (\(n=8,K=2,p=\tfrac12\), naive: \(\rho_{20}=0,.455,.447,.409,.365\) for the five \(q\)). Mechanism: a mutated candidate starts from the already-overfit vector and need only flip one wrong item without losing a right one, so improvements keep arriving when an independent redraw would almost never beat an inflated \(c\) (hill-climbing the fixed item set); the 0.1 remark that correlation lowers every per-generation inflation was wrong beyond \(G=1\). Synthetic mechanism result.

6. True gains: the drift chain

State \((c,i)\) with \(p(i)=\min(1,\tfrac14+i/8)\). Each candidate draws a gain multiple \(m\) from a law (constant, or one law per generation), has probability \(p(i+m)\), and on acceptance the state becomes \((M,i+m^*)\), \(m^*\) the accepted candidate's gain; by exchangeability \(P(M=j,m^*=m)=P(M=j)\,q_m\,\mathrm{Bin}(n,p(i+m))(j)/\mathrm{mix}(j)\). Reported \(\mathbb E[c_g]/n\), true \(\mathbb E[p(i_g)]\), phantom share \(1-\)true gain/reported gain. Verified by enumeration at \(n=2,K=2,G=2\) (six laws, two policies); the zero-gain law reproduces the null chain exactly (test). Laws: fixed \(\delta=\tfrac18\); two-point \(\delta=\tfrac18\) w.p. \(\tfrac14\); decaying two-point \(q_g=\tfrac14(\tfrac34)^{g-1}\), a lattice analogue of the simulation's \(0.06\cdot0.95^{g-1}\). Cells \((n,K)\in\{(4,2),(4,4),(8,2),(8,4),(16,2)\}\), \(G=20\), naive and \(z=1\). Everything below is a synthetic mechanism result on those cells unless marked otherwise.

  • Two-point, naive, \(n=8,K=2\): reported \(0.778\), true \(0.420\); phantom share \(0.678\) over \(0..20\), \(0.711\) early half, \(0.504\) late half; late/early gain ratio reported \(0.195\), true \(0.334\). Decaying: phantom \(0.838\) whole, \(0.977\) late; late/early reported \(0.130\) versus true \(0.016\).
  • Against results.json: P3 (late phantom share above one half under naive reuse) holds on every cell with \(n\le8\) for both laws and on all cells for the decaying law; P5 (reported late/early ratio at least twice the true one) holds only under the decaying schedule (about 8× here, 4× in the simulation) and reverses on every constant-gain cell (an inflated count is beaten less often by no-gain candidates, so late acceptances are enriched for real gains): P5 is a property of the decaying gain scale.
  • Gate ordering with true gains: fixed gain, \(z=1\) gap above naive on every cell (\(n=8,K=2\): \(0.347\) vs \(0.279\)); decaying gain, gate below naive on every cell. Proposition 4 is a null result and must not be read as "gating reduces the gap". Lattice ceiling: with fixed gain at \(n=16\) the late phantom share is negative (reported score pinned near one while \(p\) still rises); phantom shares near the ceiling are not interpretable.

Proposition 7 (one generation, fixed gain). From \((c,p_{\rm inc})\) with every candidate at \(p_{\rm inc}+\delta\) and \(M\) the max of \(K\) counts, one generation changes the gap by \(\mathbb E[((M-c)/n-\delta)\,1_{\rm accept}]\). Since \(1\{M>c\}-1\{M>c+t\}=1\{c<M\le c+t\}\), \[\Delta\rho_{\rm naive}-\Delta\rho_{\rm gate}=\mathbb E\big[((M-c)/n-\delta)\,1\{c<M\le c+t(c)\}\big],\] and on the window \(1\le M-c\le t(c)\). Hence (i) \(\delta\le1/n\): integrand \(\ge0\), the gate lowers the one-generation gap change; (ii) \(\delta\ge t(c)/n\): integrand \(\le0\), the gate raises it; (iii) otherwise the sign depends on the law of \(M\) on the window. Algebraic identity (one_step_gap_change). Checked on all 270 states \((c,i<\text{top})\) of the five \(z=1\) cells: difference equals window integral exactly and has the predicted sign everywhere (\(n=16\): 65 strictly negative, 37 zero). Case (iii) takes both signs (\(n=16,K=2,c=8,t=2,\delta=\tfrac3{32}\): \(-0.0015\) at \(p_{\rm inc}=\tfrac14\), \(+0.0025\) at \(\tfrac12\)). At \(n=8,z=1\), \(t(c)\le1\) and \(\delta=\tfrac18=1/n=t/n\), so both cases hold, the one-step difference is identically zero on all 54 states, \(\rho_1\) agrees, and the \(G=20\) ordering (gate above) is a state-occupation effect: the gated chain lags where the gap is still positive while the naive chain nears the corner \((n,\text{top})\) where it vanishes. At \(n=16\) case (ii) holds strictly and the gate is above naive at every \(g\ge1\) (the DP's increments match the formula, drift_gap_increment_from_one_step, \(g\le2\)). Chaining, finite. Fixed gain, \(\delta\in\{\tfrac1{16},\tfrac18,\tfrac14\}\), five cells (15 pairs): case (ii) at every state on 4 pairs, gate above naive at every \(g=1..20\) (76/76); case (i) on 7 pairs, 114/133 later generations agree, the sign reversing at \(g=15,9\) (\(n=4,\delta=\tfrac18,K=2,4\)) and \(g=20\) (\(n=16,\delta=\tfrac1{16}\)); both cases on 4 pairs, \(\rho_1\) identical then gate above from \(g=2\) or 3. \(G=1\) agrees on all 15 as the theorem requires; beyond that the sign need not chain.

Proposition 8 (dominance in true value). Fixed gain (one lattice step per acceptance; a multiple \(m\) is the same chain on the lattice \(p_0+im\delta\)), any non-decreasing level function \(p\), any \(n,K\), any threshold rule \(t\ge0\), any state \(x=(c,i)\) and any non-decreasing \(\varphi\) on levels: \(\mathbb E^t_x[\varphi(i_g)]\le\mathbb E^0_x[\varphi(i_g)]\) for every \(g\). In particular \(\mathbb E[p(i_g)]\) is ordered and the gated level is stochastically dominated, from every initial law. Proof. \(P,Q\) the naive and gated kernels, \(u_k=P^k\varphi\), \(w_k=Q^k\varphi\), \(a(c)=\min(n,c+t(c))\), \(M_\ell\) the best of \(K\) candidates at level \(\ell\) (stochastically non-decreasing in \(\ell\)), \(F_\ell,\pi_\ell\) its CDF and law. (i) Naive monotonicity: \(u_k(c,i)\ge u_k(c+1,i)\) and \(u_k(c,i+1)\ge u_k(c,i)\) for all \(k\), by downward induction on the level. The first follows from the second at \((c+1,i)\): on the same draws the chains from \((c,i)\) and \((c+1,i)\) agree until a draw equals \(c+1\), which sends the lower to \((c+1,i+1)\) and leaves the upper at \((c+1,i)\), so \(u_k(c,i)-u_k(c+1,i)=\sum_sF_i(c)^{s-1}\pi_i(c+1)[u_{k-s}(c+1,i+1)-u_{k-s}(c+1,i)]\). The second follows from the first at level \(i+2\): expand the lower chain two jumps and the upper one. The upper waits less (success probability \(1-F_{i+1}(c)\) is larger), lands stochastically below the lower's second landing (\(M_{i+1}\,|\,{>}c\preceq M_{i+1}\,|\,{>}x\) for the lower's first landing \(x>c\)), \(\varphi(i+1)\le\varphi(i+2)\le u(\cdot,i+2)\), and \(u(\cdot,i+2)\) is non-increasing in the count and non-decreasing in time; a lower chain whose first landing is \(n\) keeps \(\varphi(i+1)\). Base: at the top levels \(u_k\) is constant in \(c\), one level below it is \(\varphi+(\varphi_{\rm top}-\varphi)(1-F(c)^k)\). (ii) Suppressed state: \(w_k(c,i)\le u_k(j,i+1)\) for \(c<j\le a(c)\). One gated step: the self-loop uses (ii) at \(k-1\); each landing \(J>a(c)\ge j\) uses (iii) at \(k-1\) and then (i) to pull \(u_{k-1}(J,i+1)\) up to \(u_{k-1}(j,i+1)\); \(u_{k-1}\le u_k\) closes. (iii) Dominance: on the same draws from \(x\) the chains agree until the naive one accepts a draw \(M\in(c,a(c)]\) the gate rejects, and the remaining difference \(u_{k-s}(M,i+1)-w_{k-s}(c,i)\) is \(\ge0\) by (ii). Induct on \(k\). Algebraic identity; the limit version is the backward induction \(V_Q\le V_P\), last step by (i). Checked (dominance_structure_fixture) at every state, horizon \(\le25\) and in the exact limit on \((n,K)\in\{(4,2),(8,2),(16,2),(8,4)\}\), \(\delta\in\{\tfrac1{16},\tfrac18,\tfrac14\}\), \(z\in\{1,2,3\}\) and a non-gate rule, plus two non-uniform lattices; (i) also holds for \(\varphi=1\{i\ge\ell\}\). The naive hypothesis is needed: for \(t=(0,2,0,0)\le t'=(1,2,0,1)\) on \(n=3,K=1\), levels \((\tfrac1{32},\tfrac5{32},\tfrac9{16},\tfrac{23}{32},\tfrac{15}{16})\), the higher rule is ahead from \((0,0)\) in the limit (\(1483/2752\) vs \(9175/41872\): the lower rule lands at count 1, which \(t=2\) absorbs) and at horizon 3 from \((0,2)\).

The count: search. \(\mathbb E^0[c_\infty]=n\) whenever \(p(1)>0\), so the limit ordering is trivial. Exact search (dominance_sweep_fixture) over \(n\in\{4,8,16\}\), \(K\in\{1,2,4\}\), \(z\in\{1,2,3\}\), \(\delta\in\{\tfrac1{16},\tfrac18,\tfrac14,\tfrac38\}\), \(p_0\in\{\tfrac14,\tfrac12,0\}\), fixed, two-point and decaying laws: 972 cells, \(g\le100\) (stopping at absorbed mass \(\ge1-10^{-9}\)), 88,171 comparisons of \(\mathbb E[c_g]\) and \(\mathbb E[p(i_g)]\), exact limits on the 648 constant-law cells: naive above gate everywhere. The fixed-law rows are now covered by Prop. 9; the two-point and decaying rows remain finite; the mutation chain was not searched.

Proposition 9 (dominance in count). Fixed gain, any non-decreasing level function, any \(n,K\), any threshold rule \(t\ge0\), any state \(x\) and any non-decreasing \(\psi\) on counts: \(\mathbb E^t_x[\psi(c_g)]\le\mathbb E^0_x[\psi(c_g)]\) for every \(g\), hence from every initial law (stochastic dominance of the count); indeed one gated step followed by naive steps is already dominated, \(QP^k\psi\le P^{k+1}\psi\). Proof. \(\psi\) is a non-negative combination of \(1\{c\ge m\}\), so fix \(m\), \(u_k=P^k1\{c\ge m\}\). Under the naive rule \(c_k\ge m\) iff \(c_0\ge m\) or some draw by generation \(k\) was \(\ge m\) (Prop. 1), so \(u_k\) is a first-passage probability; on states \(c<m\) write \(s_k=1-u_k\), \(H(i)=1-F_{i+1}(m-1)^K\) for the probability that a draw from level \(i\) is \(\ge m\) (non-decreasing in \(i\)), \(A=F_{i+1}(c)^K\), \(\pi(J)\) for the surviving landings \(c<J<m\), \(B=\sum\pi\), \(e_k\) the \(\pi\)-average of \(s_k\) over them; so \(s_{k+1}(c,i)=As_k(c,i)+Be_k\). (L1, new better than used) \(s_{k+1}(x)\le(1-H(x))s_k(x)\), i.e. \(e_k\le s_k(x)\): by induction on \(k\), (L1) at the landings (level \(i+1\), death rate \(H(i+1)\ge H(i)\)) gives \(e_k\le(1-H(i+1))e_{k-1}\), and (L1) at \(x\) gives \(s_k(x)\ge(A+B)e_{k-1}=(1-H(i))e_{k-1}\ge e_k\); base \(s_1=1-H\). (L2, count) \(s_k(c,i)\le s_k(c+1,i)\) for \(c+1<m\): the chains share draws and agree unless the draw equals \(c+1\), which sends the lower to \((c+1,i+1)\) and leaves the upper at \((c+1,i)\); the difference is \(A[\cdot]_{k-1}+ \pi(c+1)[s_{k-1}(c+1,i)-s_{k-1}(c+1,i+1)]\ge0\) by (L2), (L3) at \(k-1\). (L3, level) \(s_k(c,i+1)\le s_k(c,i)\): with \(\sigma_r=s_r(c,i+1)\), (L2) at level \(i+1\) bounds every surviving landing of the lower chain below by \(\sigma\), so \(s_k(c,i)\ge R_k\), \(R_0=1\), \(R_k=AR_{k-1}+B\sigma_{k-1}\) (wait at \((c,i)\), then run the upper chain), and \(R_k\ge(1-H(i))\sigma_{k-1}\ge(1-H(i+1))\sigma_{k-1}\ge\sigma_k\) by (L1) at \((c,i+1)\). (L4, window) for every \(a\in(c,n]\), \(W_a=\sum_{c<J\le a}\pi_{i+1}(J)[u_k(J,i+1)-u_k(c,i)]\ge0\): terms with \(J\ge m\) are \(\ge0\); for the rest, by (L2) the \(\pi\)-average of \(s_k(\cdot,i+1)\) over \((c,\min(a,m-1)]\) is at most its average over \((c,m)\), which is \(e_k\le s_k(c,i)\) by (L1). Since \((Pf-Qf)(c,i)=W_{a(c)}\), (L4) is \(Qu_k\le Pu_k\) for every rule, and \(Q^kf=Q(Q^{k-1}f)\le Q(P^{k-1}f)\le P^kf\) closes. At the top level all lemmas are equalities. Algebraic identity; it uses only that the best of \(K\) is stochastically non-decreasing in the level and that acceptance raises the level by one. Checked (count_dominance_lemma_fixture): (L1)–(L4) and each intermediate inequality on 23 cells (\(n\le16\), \(K\le4\); uniform, non-uniform and big-jump lattices), every \(m\) and state (6,168 below \(m\)), horizon \(\le12\); dominance for all 120 rules at \(n=4\) (test).

Why the standard routes fail, with witnesses. The pointwise cone \(u_k(J,i+1)\ge u_k(c,i)\) is false: \(n=4,K=2,\delta=\tfrac1{16}\), from \((0,2)\) at horizon 5 the landing \((3,3)\) is worth \(3.4756<3.4840\), so rejecting the draw 3 once and then playing naive beats the naive rule at horizon 6: the naive rule maximizes \(\mathbb E[c_g]\) among threshold rules, not among all accept/reject policies.
