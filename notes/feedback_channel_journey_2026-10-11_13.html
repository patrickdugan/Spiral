The feedback-channel programme: a journey log for auditors

Written 2026-10-13 by the Claude Code session that produced the branch feedback-channel-and-rsi-atlas. This document is the map; the territory is the session log under docs/session_logs/2026-10-11_feedback_channel/ (conversation transcript, sixteen subagent transcripts, tool results), narrowed to this investigation and redacted as its README describes. Every claim below can be checked against a commit on the branch and a receipt under output/.

1. What was asked

  1. 2026-10-11. Patrick forwarded a patch produced by a separate cloud session: a Feedback operator for the evaluation design calculus, an atlas applying it to five published takeoff claims, and two seeded toy simulations. He asked whether it could be pushed to the public repository.
  2. 2026-10-12. He asked whether the work had drifted from his aim, the mathematics of evaluation notation, and then set the research question: whether Will Brown's reinforcement-learning paradigm is overestimated, approached through the training signal as a declared feedback channel, with topology, decision theory, graph theory on Markov decision chains, complexity, and the white-boxing of evaluation design as falsifiable axioms. He asked for subagents on the open problems.
  3. "lattice and push", then "proceed in that order" (five items), then "proceed" (four items), then "proceed, don't worry about Will" (five items), then "proceed, also upload the log" (this document and the raw export), then "cont" after Patrick switched the session's model from claude-fable-5-1 to claude-opus-5-5 when the Fable usage limit had stopped two subagents (§3).

Will Brown's posts of 2026-10-12 were never read: X walls the profile, the Nitter mirrors are shut down, and the syndication endpoint rate-limits anonymous requests. The signal note cites his July 2026 post and a 2025 interview instead and says so.

3. How the work was organised

The session acted as lead. Mathematical problems that were independent of each other were delegated to subagents, one per problem, each in its own detached git worktree at the branch head, with instructions to create or extend only its own files, never to commit, and to report what was proven in general versus checked on enumerated cases. The lead then reran the agent's tests, inspected its receipt and git status, read its note, and in several cases its proof line by line, before copying the files into the branch worktree, running the full suite, committing and pushing. The subagent transcripts in the export are the primary record of each delegation; the lead's verification of each is in the main transcript immediately after the agent's report.

4. Errors found and corrected, in the order they were found

An auditor should weigh these more than the theorems.

  1. The forwarded atlas (draft 0.1) had been written from a summarizing fetch tool. The line-level check found: OpenAI's Lean share was 42% (300/719), not 162/722, and three manuscripts had been withdrawn the day after release; the OpenAI run had within-run feedback and a mid-run model swap the atlas called "not stated"; Anthropic's index weights split each sampled person-week evenly across tasks, so the atlas's "fast tasks weigh less" mechanism did not follow; METR's own doubling time was 128.7 days, the atlas's 131/89 came from a prediction-market page; the AIDE² contrast of 1.90 standard errors was cross-model, while the fixed-model gains were 2.6–7.4. Commit 662557c.
  2. The toy-simulation README claimed byte-for-byte reproduction; on this machine one of three result files differs in the last digit of 24 floats. Recorded as such.
  3. Multigen 0.1's boundary remark said correlated candidates lower every per-generation inflation. The exact mutation model showed the opposite for the accumulated gap. Commit 4401d78.
  4. A reading of the gate-vs-naive cells attributed the "gate above naive" ordering at n = 8 to the one-step theorem; the one-step difference is identically zero there and the ordering is a timing effect. Commit 4401d78.
  5. Lattice 0.2's claim that greedy-by-rate is optimal at one hit, with an exchange-argument proof, was false: the argument never considered staying in a category whose rate rose after a miss, and the claim fails at size 7, one past the exhaustive grid that had "verified" it. The correct statement is Theorem 10 (the best finisher, in closed form). The lead had repeated the false claim in its own summaries. Commit a5ab4ff.
  6. Two regularities that held on the exhaustive grid (equal rates favour the larger category; the lowest of three rates is never uniquely optimal) failed just beyond it. Commit a5ab4ff.
  7. A formula the lead supplied to a subagent (de Moivre's mean absolute deviation) was off by a factor of two; the subagent caught it by enumeration before using it. Commit 70f6f27.
  8. A claim in a subagent brief that the controller–judge agreement rate stops being invariant under label-dependent gold flips was wrong; the agent showed C⊕F = J⊕F iff C = J for any flip law. Commit f1b9dad.
  9. Two citations in the crosswalk were taken from memory and are marked unverified in the note rather than presented as checked.
  10. The mixed-gain agent's note said lemmas L1–L3 hold under every mixed gain law. Its own module proves L2–L3 only for gains in {0, 1}. The lead narrowed the sentence before committing. Commit fe9d51c.
  11. The interrupted fourth-rater code had six defects, found by the agent that finished it. It rounded the three agreement counts separately, giving counts no item table can produce. It expected the parameter Jacobian's rank to drop with a chance rater, which it does not. It misoriented an anti-informative second grader. It did not withhold the joint statement when the constraint check failed, as its docstring said it would. It asserted disjoint ε₁ ranges at m = 742, which is false. It had an off-by-one in a tail law. Commit 988efa5.
  12. The lead's brief for the completion agent named the wrong note file. The agent found the right one from the original brief.
  13. The first version of the session log, published on 2026-10-13, left 25 JSON records unparseable. Its memory-line rule ran on raw text, and where a private review's line ended a string, the rule ran past the closing quote and deleted the rest of the record. Its README said every line parsed and nothing else changed, which was false for those records. The lead found this while refreshing the log and rewrote the redaction to work on decoded strings.
  14. The same first version missed one private audit's working-tree folder name, which appears 24 times in it. The refreshed log removes it, and the history rewrite removed that version from the branch.
  15. Both earlier versions of the log carried the author's other work. Every turn's context held the full memory index, with a line for each of the author's other projects, and a git-status snapshot of the whole working tree. The lead had redacted only the private-review lines and published the rest. The author pointed out that the log was meant to cover this investigation only. The current version withholds every memory-index line except this investigation's own notes, the git-status snapshots, other projects' file names, five memory notes read from other work, and the app's configuration files. The author then had the branch history rewritten so that neither earlier version remains on it.

The lesson recorded in the lead's memory: small exhaustive grids let two false theorems through; a regularity observed on a grid is stressed at larger sizes before anything is called a proof.

5. The results an auditor should test first

  • Theorem 10 (lattice): one-hit optimum = min_c (n_c+1)/(F_c+1). Witness that rate-greedy fails: {(2,1),(16,9)}, 3/2 against 53/32.
  • Proposition 7 (lattice): no index rule; witness {(2,1),(4,2)} with and without a (1,1) category at two hits.
  • Theorem B (selection KL): KL(π_K‖p₀) ≤ ln K − (K−1)/K for every finite law and tie rule, via jitter and Jensen.
  • Propositions 8 and 9 (multigen): the gate lowers the true value and the count in stochastic order, for every threshold rule, under fixed gains.
  • L4 (signal): observed accuracy = a − ε + 2(1−a)ε₁; the ceiling is reached without memorization iff ε₁ ≥ 1/2.
  • The mutation-model finding (multigen 0.2): correlation with the incumbent lowers first-generation inflation and raises the accumulated gap.
  • The corollary in gold calibration 0.1: noisy gold makes an independent judge look correlated by exactly η(1−η)(2a−1)(1−2ε).
  • Proposition 6 (gold calibration 0.3): four raters give exactly six equalities, via a lower-triangular minor with determinant −c₁₂⁴c₁₃². A grader copying the gold grader with probability ρ leaves the residual ρV₁c₃₄ on one tetrad. The lead re-derived both by hand and checked the fixture, the six residuals and the copy residual on 200 random model points each, with a script independent of the module.
  • The distance-two closure (multigen 0.5): at count m − 2 the window sums obey W_k = A·W_{k−1} + E_{k−1} with E ≥ 0, for every gain law.
  • The margin c(K) (selection KL 0.3): exactly zero at K = 2 and 3, as the K = 2 and K = 3 theorems require, and 0.020561 at K = 4.

7. How to read the raw log

transcript.jsonl is one JSON object per line in conversation order; tool calls and their results are interleaved with the assistant's text. Each subagent file under subagents/ is the same format for one delegation; its .prefix.json holds the context it started with. tool-results/ holds tool outputs too large to inline. REDACTIONS.json lists, per file, how many replacements the redaction made; the rules are in the README beside it. A line reading [... the author's other work: withheld] held context from the author's other projects. A line or string reading [withheld by redaction: ...] named one of the private review terms; nearly all such lines are in the stretch where the redaction itself was built.
