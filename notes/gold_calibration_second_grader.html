1. Model

True label \(Y\in\{0,1\}\), \(\pi=\Pr[Y=1]\). Three raters report \(Y\) flipped independently given \(Y\), each with one symmetric error rate: the gold grader \(G_1\) (\(\eta_1\)), a second grader \(G_2\) (\(\eta_2\)) and the controller's own answer \(X_C\) (error \(1-a\)). The judge is excluded: its verdict implies a label that equals \(Y\) iff \(J=1\), and that label is conditionally independent of \(X_C\) given \(Y\) iff \(\varepsilon_0=\varepsilon\), i.e. iff \(\varepsilon_1=\varepsilon\) — axiom A2, the hypothesis under test. Write \(\tau=1-2\pi\), \(d_i=1-2e_i\), and for \(S\subseteq\{1,2,3\}\) the Walsh coefficient \(\hat p_S=E\prod_{i\in S}(-1)^{X_i}\).

Proposition 1. \(\hat p_S=\tau^{[|S|\text{ odd}]}\prod_{i\in S}d_i\). Proof. \(E[(-1)^{X_i}\mid Y]=(-1)^Y d_i\); conditional independence multiplies; \(E[(-1)^{kY}]\) is \(\tau\) for odd \(k\) and \(1\) for even. \(\square\) So the marginal bias \(M_i=\Pr[X_i=0]-\Pr[X_i=1]=\tau d_i\), the pair polarity \(\kappa_{ij}=2A_{ij}-1=d_id_j\) with \(A_{ij}=\Pr[X_i=X_j]=1-e_i-e_j+2e_ie_j\), and the parity bias \(P=\hat p_{123}=\tau d_1d_2d_3\). (walsh_coefficients, three_rater_model; checked against enumeration of the \(2^4\) latent cells \((Y,X_1,X_2,X_3)\) on a grid of 750 rational parameter points.)

2. Identities and identifiability

Proposition 2. If \(\kappa_{jk}\ne0\) then \((1-2e_i)^2=\kappa_{ij}\kappa_{ik}/\kappa_{jk}\). Proof. \(d_id_j\,d_id_k/(d_jd_k)=d_i^2\). \(\square\) (polarity_squares.) From the agreement rates alone the three \(\kappa\)'s determine the three squares and nothing more; the root's sign is fixed by the convention \(e_i<\tfrac12\), which needs every \(\kappa>0\). That convention is a modelling assumption, not a finding: \((\tau,d)\mapsto(-\tau,-d)\) — every rater worse than chance and the prior swapped — leaves every \(\hat p_S\) unchanged (three_rater_model(1-π, 1-e) equals three_rater_model(π, e) on the whole grid). A rater at chance (\(d_i=0\)) zeroes both of its polarities and leaves the other two with one equation for two unknowns (polarity_squares raises). Exactly one or three negative \(\kappa\)'s fit no symmetric model of any sign.

The root is irrational in general, so three exact operations replace it (error_rate_from_square, error_rate_at_most, error_rate_bracket): (a) exact recovery when the square is a perfect square, which at expected counts with rational rates it always is — the fixture's \(\kappa=(\tfrac{12}{25},\tfrac{12}{25},\tfrac9{25})\) give squares \(\tfrac{16}{25},\tfrac9{25},\tfrac9{25}\), hence \(\eta_1=\tfrac1{10}\), \(\eta_2=\tfrac15\), \(a=\tfrac45\); (b) membership: \(\eta_1\le\eta_0\) iff \(d_1\ge1-2\eta_0\) iff (for \(\eta_0<\tfrac12\)) \(\kappa_{12}\kappa_{13}/\kappa_{23}\ge(1-2\eta_0)^2\), a rational comparison; (c) a bracket of width \(2^{-16}\) by bisection on (b), or from \(r=\lfloor2^{16}\sqrt S\rfloor=\mathrm{isqrt}\lfloor4^{16}S\rfloor\) (root_bracket, exact integer arithmetic). Gradient of the estimate (eta_gradient): \(\partial\eta_1/\partial\kappa=(-d_1/4\kappa_{12},\,-d_1/4\kappa_{13},\,+d_1/4\kappa_{23})\), at the fixture \((-\tfrac5{12},-\tfrac5{12},\tfrac59)\); per agreement rate twice that.

3. Testable constraints and falsifiers

The \(2^3\) cells have 7 degrees of freedom; the model has 4 parameters \((\tau,d_1,d_2,d_3)\), and its exact Jacobian has rank 4 wherever every \(d_i\ne0\) (the three pair rows have determinant \(-2d_1d_2d_3\) on the \(d\) columns; model_jacobian_rank). So there are exactly three constraints:

Proposition 3. \(M_1\kappa_{23}=M_2\kappa_{13}=M_3\kappa_{12}=P\). Proof. Each side is \(\tau d_1d_2d_3\). \(\square\) Conversely, a table with all \(\kappa>0\), squares in \([0,1]\), \(M_i^2\le d_i^2\) and these three equalities is the image of exactly one parameter point with every \(e_i<\tfrac12\) (set \(d_i\) from Proposition 2, \(\tau=M_1/d_1\); the equalities make \(M_i/d_i\) agree; argued here, not separately checked in code). In cell terms: for every \(x\), \(p(x)+p(\bar x)=\tfrac14\big(1+\sum_{i<j}\kappa_{ij}\chi_{ij}(x)\big)\) holds for any distribution, so the complementary-pair sums carry no constraint; the content sits in the imbalances \(p(x)-p(\bar x)=\tfrac14\big(\sum_iM_i\chi_i(x)+P\chi_{123}(x)\big)\), whose parity term must equal each \(M_i\kappa_{jk}\). At a balanced prior every odd coefficient vanishes and the equalities read \(0=0\): the check has no power against grader dependence when \(\pi=\tfrac12\) (vacuous_at_balanced_prior).

Falsifiers (model_constraints), all exact: a table with \(\kappa=(-\tfrac15,\tfrac15,\tfrac15)\) (one negative: no sign assignment); a table with \(\kappa=(\tfrac{12}{25},\tfrac{12}{25},\tfrac1{25})\), whose implied \((1-2\eta_1)^2=\tfrac{144}{25}>1\); and a dependent grader. If \(G_2\) copies \(G_1\) with probability \(\rho\) and grades independently otherwise, then \(r_1=r_2=-\tau d_C\,\rho\,4\eta_1(1-\eta_1)\) and \(r_3=0\), where \(r_i=M_i\kappa_{jk}-P\) (copy_grader_residual; checked for \(\rho\in\{0,\tfrac14,\tfrac12,\tfrac34,1\}\) at the fixture prior and for four \(\rho\) at each of five priors): the failing constraints name the dependent pair, and any \(\rho>0\) is detected whenever \(\tau\ne0\), \(a\ne\tfrac12\) and \(0<\eta_1<1\). With asymmetric errors the model has 7 parameters, the just-identified Dawid–Skene case of §7, which has no such constraint.

4. Pipeline (calibrate)

Input: the \(2^4\) record of \((x_C,v_J,g_1,g_2)\) on \(m\) sealed items. (1) Marginalise over the judge to the label table \((g_1,g_2,x_C)\); run the constraint check. (2) \(\kappa\)'s, squares, \(\hat\eta_1,\hat\eta_2,\hat a\) by Proposition 2, exact or bracketed. (3) Tabulate the judge note's \(2\times2\) against \(G_1\): \(a',\varepsilon',w=W_1'/m\). (4) Invert Proposition 2 of the judge note at \(\eta=\hat\eta_1\) (gold_noise_inverse, reused) to \((\hat a,\hat\varepsilon,\hat\varepsilon_1)\); the inverted \(\hat a\) equals the identity's \(\hat a\) on every table with all \(\kappa>0\), since \(\hat d_1\hat d_C=\kappa_{1C}\) exactly. (5) expected_table at the recovered rates, rounded half-up (rounded_table), then independence_p_value (declared \(\varepsilon\), or the plug-in flagged), fisher_p_value, ceiling_p_value, clopper_pearson_contains. When \(\hat\eta_1\) is a bracket, every inverted rate is monotone in \(\eta\) (\(da/d\eta=(2a'-1)/(1-2\eta)^2\), \(d\varepsilon/d\eta=(2\varepsilon'-1)/(1-2\eta)^2\), and the next line), so the bracket ends map to ends and (4)–(5) run at both.

Sensitivity. Substituting \(a-\varepsilon=(a'-\varepsilon')/(1-2\eta)\) and \(1-a=(1-a'-\eta)/(1-2\eta)\) into the note's inversion gives one rational function \(\varepsilon_1(\eta)=\dfrac{w(1-2\eta)-\eta(a'-\varepsilon')}{1-a'-\eta}\) (inverted_eps1, equal to the judge module's value on the grid). Numerator and denominator are affine, so with \(K=w(2a'-1)-(a'-\varepsilon')(1-a')\), free of \(\eta\): \(\dfrac{d\varepsilon_1}{d\eta}=\dfrac{K}{(1-a'-\eta)^2}\) and exactly \(\varepsilon_1(\eta+\Delta)-\varepsilon_1(\eta)=\dfrac{\Delta K}{(1-a'-\eta)(1-a'-\eta-\Delta)}\) (eps1_sensitivity, eps1_displacement; checked against direct re-inversion). At the fixture \(K=-\tfrac{64}{625}\), \(1-a'-\eta=\tfrac4{25}\): \(d\varepsilon_1/d\eta=-4\) for the independent judge and \(-\tfrac52\) for the shared one. Overestimating \(\eta\) by \(\Delta\) lowers \(\hat\varepsilon_1\) by about \(4\Delta\) (more of the wrong-wrong cell is attributed to flips); \(\Delta=\tfrac1{100}\) moves it by exactly \(-\tfrac{16}{375}\). Chained through the gradient of §2, \(d\varepsilon_1/dA_{G_1G_2}=\tfrac{10}{3}\): a 1-point error in the graders' agreement rate moves \(\hat\varepsilon_1\) by 3.3 points.

Falsifiers of the pipeline: \(G_1\) and \(G_2\) share a rubric or source (conditional independence fails; the constraint check is the test, with no power at \(\pi=\tfrac12\)); any rater at or below chance (not identified, or the sign convention falsified); the sample touched by the loop (measurement_ledger reused; calibration_valid withholds). The tests in step (5) take the corrected counts as given: they are an estimate, not a sample, so the sampling error of \(\hat\eta_1\) is not in their p-values; §6 gives that error exactly.

6. The sampling law of \(\hat\eta_1\)

On \(m\) items the label table is \(\mathrm{Multinomial}(m;p)\); \(\hat\kappa_{ij}=(2N_{ij}-m)/m\) from the agreement counts, \(\hat S=\hat\kappa_{12}\hat\kappa_{13}/\hat\kappa_{23}\), \(\hat\eta_1=(1-\sqrt{\hat S})/2\), defined iff every \(\hat\kappa>0\) and \(\hat S\le1\). Per item \((I_{12},I_{13},I_{23})\) takes four values only — all agree, or exactly one pair — with probabilities \(q_{\text{all}}=p(000)+p(111)=\tfrac14(1+\sum\kappa)\) and \(q_{ij}=A_{ij}-q_{\text{all}}\), functions of the \(\kappa\)'s alone: the law of \(\hat\eta_1\) is free of the prior and is the image of a four-category multinomial, enumerated in \(\binom{m+3}{3}\) integer terms (sampling_law: weights over a common denominator \(D^m\), the square's class by integer comparison, its moments grouped by \(\hat\kappa_{23}\), the moments of \(\hat\eta_1\) bracketed through \(\lfloor2^{16}\sqrt{\hat S}\rfloor\) per outcome). Enumerating all \(\binom{m+7}{7}\) label tables instead (sampling_law_by_tables; 245,157 at \(m=16\), 0.7 s) gives every class probability and moment identically; the reduction reaches \(m=742\) in the receipt (68,638,340 terms; the whole receipt run, with \(m=400\) and §9 at 4000, takes about 8 minutes, most of it there). Moments are conditional on the defined event; "within" is \(\Pr[\text{defined},\ |\hat\eta_1-\eta_1|\le\tfrac1{50}]\). Truth: \(S=\tfrac{16}{25}\), \(\eta_1=\tfrac1{10}\), \(d=(\tfrac45,\tfrac35,\tfrac35)\).

7. The asymmetric model (two-class Dawid–Skene)

Rater \(i\): false-negative \(\alpha_i=\Pr[X_i=0\mid Y=1]\), false-positive \(\beta_i=\Pr[X_i=1\mid Y=0]\); with \(\pi\), seven parameters on seven degrees of freedom (dawid_skene_model). Write \(s_i=1-\alpha_i\), \(t_i=\beta_i\), \(\delta_i=1-\alpha_i-\beta_i\), \(u=\pi(1-\pi)\); from the table \(p_i=\Pr[X_i=1]\), \(c_{ij}=p_{ij}-p_ip_j\), \(k_3=E\prod(X_i-p_i)=p_{123}-\sum_ip_ip_{jk}+2p_1p_2p_3\).

Proposition 4. \(c_{ij}=u\delta_i\delta_j\), \(k_3=u(1-2\pi)\delta_1\delta_2\delta_3\), so \(R^2:=k_3^2+4c_{12}c_{13}c_{23}=u^2\delta_1^2\delta_2^2\delta_3^2\) and, under \(\delta_1\delta_2\delta_3>0\), with \(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\): \[\pi=\frac{R-k_3}{2R},\qquad s_i=p_i+\frac{R+k_3}{2c_{jk}},\qquad t_i=p_i-\frac{R-k_3}{2c_{jk}}.\] Proof. \(s_i-p_i=(1-\pi)\delta_i\) and \(t_i-p_i=-\pi\delta_i\) have product \(-u\delta_i^2=-c_{ij}c_{ik}/c_{jk}\) and sum \((1-2\pi)\delta_i=k_3/c_{jk}\): the roots of \(z^2-(k_3/c_{jk})z-c_{ij}c_{ik}/c_{jk}\), discriminant \(R^2/c_{jk}^2\); and \(u=c_{12}c_{13}c_{23}/R^2\) gives \(1-2\pi=k_3/R\). \(\square\) One root for everything: \(\pi\) is monotone and each rate affine in \(R\), so a \(2^{-16}\) bracket on \(R\) brackets all seven (dawid_skene_three_raters; exact when \(R^2\) is a rational square, always at expected counts since \(R=u|\delta_1\delta_2\delta_3|\)). Identifiable iff every \(c_{ij}\ne0\) (\(0<\pi<1\), every \(\delta_i\ne0\)) and \(R^2>0\). Label switching: \((\pi,\alpha,\beta)\) and \((1-\pi,1-\beta,1-\alpha)\) give the same table (\(\delta\mapsto-\delta\)); all_informative (\(\delta_i>0\): hit rate above false-alarm rate for every rater) needs every \(c_{ij}>0\) and refuses otherwise; rater_1_informative fixes \(\delta_1>0\), puts \(\sigma=\mathrm{sign}\,c_{23}\) in the formulas and recovers an anti-informative \(G_2\) exactly (\((\alpha_2,\beta_2)=(\tfrac35,\tfrac35)\), \(\delta_2=-\tfrac15\)). Both solutions are returned.

Fixture. \(\pi=\tfrac14\), \(\alpha=(\tfrac1{10},\tfrac15,\tfrac1{10})\), \(\beta=(\tfrac15,\tfrac1{10},\tfrac3{10})\) (gold misses 10% of positives, false-alarms on 20% of negatives; the controller is right on 90% / 70%): \(\delta=(\tfrac7{10},\tfrac7{10},\tfrac35)\), \(c=(\tfrac{147}{1600},\tfrac{63}{800},\tfrac{63}{800})\), \(k_3=\tfrac{441}{16000}\), \(R=\tfrac{441}{8000}\), every parameter exact; so on a grid of 256 points, and on the symmetric grid the fit returns \(\alpha_i=\beta_i=e_i\), the \(\eta\)'s of §2. The exact Jacobian of \((\pi,\alpha,\beta)\mapsto\) cells (affine in each parameter, so differences are exact) has rank 7 there and on the symmetric grid (5 with a chance rater): no degree of freedom is left, no equality constraint, hence no test of conditional independence — against the symmetric model's three (a fourth rater restores six: §8). Only inequalities remain: \(p(100)=p(010)=p(001)=\tfrac7{30}\), \(p(011)=p(101)=p(110)=\tfrac1{10}\) has \(R^2=-\tfrac{49}{30000}\), no real point (outside_model); and rates must lie in \([0,1]\). The copy grader of §5 passes both: at \(\rho=1\) the fit is exact and in range — \(\pi=\tfrac3{10}\), \(\alpha_1=\beta_1=\alpha_2=\beta_2=0\), controller \((\tfrac7{20},\tfrac{31}{140})\): two perfect graders and an asymmetric controller — while the symmetric constraints fail; \(\rho=\tfrac12\) likewise (bracketed). The dependence §3 detects is absorbed as asymmetry. A fixture table with \(\tfrac1{100}\) moved between two cells is bracketed and in range.

Asymmetric inversion (asymmetric_gold_noise_inverse, calibrate_asymmetric). With \(a_y\) the controller's accuracy on class \(y\) (\(1-\alpha_3\), \(1-\beta_3\)), \(f_y\) the gold flip rate (\(\alpha_1\), \(\beta_1\)), the judge depending on correctness only, measured against \(G_1\): \(a'=\sum_y\pi_y(a_y(1-f_y)+(1-a_y)f_y)\), \(\varepsilon'=\sum_y\pi_y(f_y+(1-2f_y)(a_y\varepsilon_0+(1-a_y)\varepsilon_1))\), \(w=\sum_y\pi_y((1-a_y)(1-f_y)\varepsilon_1+a_yf_y(1-\varepsilon_0))\). The agreement rate \(\Pr[C=J]\) is still invariant: \(C\oplus F=J\oplus F\) iff \(C=J\), whatever the law of \(F\).
