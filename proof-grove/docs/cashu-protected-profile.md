# Protected Cashu profile and campaign evidence

The protected prototype gives a holder a Bitcoin recovery path when the claim is issued. At issuance the wallet verifies that a confirmed reserve outpoint pays the agreed holder-controlled script. In the November 23 melt-funded fixture, CDK first accepted and spent an existing Cashu bundle to fund the recovery outpoint. The mint could refuse that initial melt. After funding the holder could exit independently in both models.

## PG-ZK-02 — Nova accounting

`integrations/cashu-nova` folds a uniform Pallas/Vesta step. The public state contains four domain-separated Poseidon trees (reserve, intents, live claims, spent claims), three integer balances, sequence, journal and ledger context. The public verifier starts at a pinned genesis or independently accepted checkpoint and uses the expected final state and exact step count. The journal binds the previous journal root, context, sequence, operation, identifiers, amounts and admission-evidence digest. Applications must provide those evidence records and make witness data available for independent replay.

| Step | Authenticated state updates |
| --- | --- |
| Register | Require vacant reserve and intent leaves. Bind the funded outpoint and its amount to one pending intent. |
| Issue | Open the pending intent at its exact amount and reserve, leave an intent tombstone, and create a vacant live claim whose spent slot is empty. |
| Split | Open and consume a live claim, set its spent leaf, and create two distinct positive claims with the same reserve binding and conserved value. |
| Swap | Open and consume one live claim, set its spent leaf, and create another claim at the same amount and reserve. |
| Redeem | Consume a live claim once and decrease its reserve and total liabilities by the exact amount. The reserve entry remains occupied, including at a zero balance. |

The circuit enforces Boolean operation selection and Merkle directions, full 256-bit identifier commitments via two range-checked limbs, authenticated empty leaves, satoshi bounds, and reserve coverage for both pending issuance and live liabilities. The eight-bit tree route is a fixture limit. Colliding occupied slots cause refusal, and production-scale deployment needs a tree design that handles collisions and tombstone growth.

The accounting proof covers the declared protected ledger. A coordinator must independently check credible Bitcoin Core or chain-verifier evidence for unspent funding and mature, confirmed withdrawals. The circuit binds evidence digests and leaves Bitcoin consensus, Cashu DLEQ, and holder signing to the admission layer. A mint with signing keys can sign additional bearer notes outside the ledger. A protected wallet must verify the canonical protected checkpoint, live claim membership, funding script, individual collateral balance, and the certificate tying that note bundle to its reserve. Missing or stale evidence fails admission. A public enumerable or witness-available protected issuance journal allows auditors to reconstruct that ledger. Completeness for a mint's external bearer liabilities cannot be deduced from an authenticated subset of the ledger.

The fixture demonstrates accounting splits and swaps. A holder assigned only a fractional bookkeeping claim has no independent ability to spend part of a Bitcoin UTXO. Protected transfers and splits require the current holder to roll the whole input into fresh reserve outputs for the new holders, or an independently enforceable transaction graph that creates those outputs. Each recipient waits for its own usable reserve and verifies its own key and recovery delay.

## PG-ZK-03 — Bitcoin claim consumption

Each protected claim controls one full outpoint. The holder's wallet stores the descriptor, funding transaction and outpoint, key and sequence, chain context, claim-bundle commitment, expected amount, and mint profile identity. A certificate binds these fields to issuance and appears in the accounting journal. The mint's on-chain cooperative redemption needs the holder signature and uses the same outpoint as the unilateral CSV exit. The holder only signs an exact authorized transaction with `SIGHASH_ALL`, covering beneficiary, amounts, change and fees. Confirmation consumes the outpoint. A replacement before finality remains subject to the same consensus exclusivity, so clients keep it pending and recompute active-chain state after a reorg.

The historical v1 fixture uses `wsh(and_v(v:pk(H),or_i(pk(M),older(6))))` with distinct holder and mint keys. New v2 issuance uses `wsh(and_v(v:sha256(C),and_v(v:pk(H),or_i(pk(M),older(delay)))))`. The public preimage of `C` commits the claim ID, chain genesis, both keys, beneficiary, funding amount, fee, delay and confirmation policy. A second desk given the same outpoint and changed terms derives a different funding script and rejects it. Both PSBTs include the preimage. The cooperative branch requires both keys and the CSV branch requires the holder key after maturity. Existing v1 bundles remain recoverable. Identical-term duplicate signing still requires canonical claim assignment, and Bitcoin permits one spend of the outpoint. [BIP68](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0068.mediawiki), [BIP112](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0112.mediawiki), [BIP174](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0174.mediawiki)

The V3 profile commits the exact ordered blinded outputs, including each denomination and keyset ID, under the issuance intent in the funding script. The wallet freezes the batch before reserve funding and derives the permanent P2PK lock from that intent. The desk recomputes the batch commitment before it can authorize a mint call. An independent observer pins the V3 bundle and mint keys, checks each note's DLEQ signature, and reblinds each note to verify the committed output. A mint can sign another set of valid notes. A different set cannot open the same committed batch under the hash-to-curve and discrete-log assumptions. Global reserve completeness and the assignment of a batch across different outpoints still require a shared canonical journal. [NUT-11](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/blob/main/11.md), [NUT-12](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/blob/main/12.md)

Undisclosed notes and global reserve completeness remain outside its statement.

The signing wallet and broadcaster are separate from these commands.

## PG-ZK-04 — BitVM3 target

The M14 example sizes a fixed settlement record, using the existing BLAKE3 and Boolean arithmetic gadgets. The record commits the network via genesis, chain anchor, old and new roots, claim ID, exact funding outpoint, reserve and beneficiary script hashes, holder authorization commitment, payout txid and wtxid, heights and CSV sequence, and reserve, payout, change and fees. The verifier checks the challenge digest, state change, block-based delay, integer ranges and exact conservation. The challenger needs the authenticated expected digest as an input. In a deployment it must be pinned by the setup and derived from the same verified proof statement, or the check would only enforce consistency with a digest chosen by the attacker.

## Measurement and evidence

The paired Cashu benchmark cost $0.2487. The issuance and 24,750-sat payout prover stages took 240.505 seconds and 257.603 seconds and peaked at 45,576,180 KiB and 46,434,300 KiB. This was a 16-thread CPU run on Hyperstack, hosted on an A100 VM. The available WSL machine reported approximately 15 GiB RAM and zero swap. Any new paid run needs ready inputs, approved spend and a bounded lease. Completion ETA is unknown.

The local RTX 3050 trial uses a 16 MiB Cashu evidence corpus and the SP1 SHA-256 guest to measure core-shard proving. Two full-CUDA attempts stopped during initialization at the device memory guard. The second sampled 3,904 MiB in use and 59 MiB free. The CUDA-core, CPU-recursion worker reached its isolated 6 GiB host RAM cap before the GPU processed a shard. The guard recorded zero swap activity and zero verified proofs. These attempts incurred zero rental charges and provide no measured proving throughput.

The CUDA Core worker initialized successfully with per-chip AIR flattening and omitted recursion setup. It recorded 1,432 new shards in 1,297 seconds, with 1,082 MiB peak sampled VRAM, 48.6 percent mean GPU utilization, and zero swap I/O. The RAM cap increased from 4 to 5 GiB while the Windows reserve stayed in force. The guard stopped the worker after WSL memory pressure rose following a file-cache reclaim. It retained 1,432 progress entries for replay. The run had zero independently verified whole proofs when it stopped. SP1 generates work dynamically, so the reported rate gives no remaining shard count or prototype ETA.

The disk-backed worker assembled a 27-shard Core proof for a 64 KiB prefix of the same corpus. An independent stock verifier accepted its pinned SHA-256 output and rejected a changed public digest and trailing proof data. The worker was then restarted on the 16 MiB input with its existing run manifest and checkpoint directory.

The 16 MiB resume replayed all 1,432 existing shards and added 952 new ones before Windows reached its RAM reserve. It kept roughly 0.53 GiB of anonymous memory in the sampled cgroup, and Linux reported no swap activity or OOM kills in the measured run. The next 2 GiB resume replayed 325 shards at less than one per second while the D: drive backing WSL had a queue depth of 26. A resumed copy verified all 2,384 shard memos on C: while preserving the source checkpoint. The complete 16 MiB proof remains outstanding.

Two short RTX A6000 spot leases used the existing ZK Rusto disk-backed CUDA prover. The 1 MiB digest control proved 25 core shards and eight recursion tasks in 14.35 seconds. SP1 accepted the compressed proof against the independently computed public digest. The first Nova trial spent its remaining allowance in a separate guest execution and produced zero shards. The second trial streamed the same Cashu Nova accounting guest directly into the prover. It completed 604 core shards and 186 recursion tasks over 312 measured seconds. GPU utilization averaged 68.93 percent with 16,426 MiB peak VRAM and 8.50 GiB peak cgroup RAM. Both trials recorded zero swap activity. The stream reached its time limit before producing a complete proof or a total task count, so completion ETA remains unknown. Both VMs and temporary storage buckets were deleted. Organization credit dropped by $0.108586 across the two lease windows. The terminated-resource endpoint had yet to return final VM line items.

The V2 reserve also passed the native Bitcoin Core 31.0 regtest. With the mint signer unloaded, the holder spent a 25,000-sat reserve after six blocks and received 24,750 sats. Core rejected the early, conflicting and replayed spends, the substituted beneficiary, and an altered issuance preimage. A separate observer could not attach the same coin to plans that changed the claim set, beneficiary or fee. The test includes a funding reorganization and recalculates maturity for the re-mined outpoint.

The SP1 Cashu guest builds separately with the pinned SP1 v6.9.0 toolchain. Its verifier takes the Nova key, compressed proof, independently replayed journal digest, step count, and initial and final state. The ELF compiled under a 1 GiB cap, and the exported verifier key matched the native receipt. Separate guest execution reached a 1.5 GiB memory cap before returning public output. The pinned exact historical Cairo payout requires its own proof and verifier.

The SP1 settlement guest binds the 404-byte V3 record to the same compressed Nova proof. It recomputes the final redemption event from the record, including the prior root, reserve outpoint, consumed claim, and payout admission. An independent observer pins the record after checking Bitcoin. The guest compiled under a 768 MiB build cap. The separate native fixture test accepted the real V3 journal and rejected record, opening, and ledger-context mutations. A fresh SP1 settlement proof and its M14 wrap remain outstanding.

SP1's pinned LightProver derived the settlement program key from the saved ELF in 51.82 seconds at 701,820 KiB peak RSS with zero swap. The M13/M14 expected statement pairs this key with the exact staged public SHA-256, wrap key, and recursion root. The pin builder rejected seven substitutions. The packed-key decoder also reproduced the key from the archived verified CUDA control.

The A6000 V3 settlement lease saved 750 shard and 232 recursion memos for the exact ELF and five pinned inputs. It measured 2.561 completed tasks per second over 383 seconds at 70.36 percent mean GPU utilization, 16,234 MiB peak VRAM, and zero swap. The lease and temporary bucket were deleted after the checkpoint transfer. The teardown-based accrual estimate was at most $0.073184. Proof completion and the total task count remain unknown.

The warm A6000 lease retained the original nonce and replayed 750 core memos. It produced 787 new core memos at 2.079 per second over 378 seconds, with 16,426 MiB peak VRAM, 71.24 percent mean GPU utilization, and zero swap. A recursion task rejected a generated reduction proof and the reduction counter then stopped advancing. The checkpoint contains 1,537 core and 239 recursion memos for diagnosis. Zero complete proofs passed verification. Hyperstack confirmed VM and bucket deletion. The accrual upper estimate was $0.073040 and the organization credit fell by $0.047534. The provider had yet to return a final line item. The rate measures core work and cannot establish a completion ETA for the proof.

A separate stock SP1 LightProver accepted the archived A6000 digest control in 35.87 seconds at 594,608 KiB peak RSS. It rejected a changed public digest and trailing proof bytes. The settlement checkpoint retains the original run nonce for resumed CUDA proving.

ZK Rusto's disk-backed sharder supports CUDA and CPU workers for SP1. The pinned Shinigami fixture uses Cairo's Stwo CPU/SIMD prover. More GPU memory can accommodate larger SP1 shards. To measure acceleration of the historical payout proof, first prepare a Stwo accelerator that emits proof bytes the pinned Shinigami verifier accepts.
